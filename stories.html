0The playground

Stories 1 to 8 use the same file: the BD TOPO 3.5 of Haute-Garonne, exactly as the French mapping agency (IGN) distributes it for free on the Géoplateforme, in a single 2.9 GB GeoPackage with 53 layers. Nothing was clipped beforehand: FilterMate scopes the territory every time.

The common thread. Every story follows the same three beats: scope the territory with a first filter (a commune or an EPCI as source layer, every other layer as target), ask the business question with a second filter whose source is an already scoped layer, then read the result in the Layers panel and on the map. The timings quoted come from filtermate.log. Hardware and cache conditions were not recorded: these observations describe individual runs, not a reproducible benchmark.
One rule to know. On GeoPackage, a new spatial filter applied to a target layer replaces that layer's previous spatial filter; the AND combine operator only applies to an existing attribute filter, or to the source layer. Stories 2, 3 and 4 take advantage of it (the question deliberately spills over the commune boundary), story 6 works around it with an attribute scope.

1The commune dossier in three minutes

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Saint-Gaudens.

The need

Camille, a project officer in a consultancy, is answering a tender from the town of Saint-Gaudens. She has the complete department GeoPackage and must deliver tonight only what concerns the commune: buildings, roads, hydrography, vegetation, facilities. With styles, in a file the client opens with a double-click.

The FilterMate translation

The commune is the source layer, picked by name in single selection. Every other layer is ticked at once in Layers to filter, with the Intersect predicate and no buffer. The Exporting tab then writes a GeoPackage with embedded project, QML styles and a ZIP archive.

  1. Load the 13 layers and open FilterMate. The Layers panel shows the counts of the whole department: 1,173,829 buildings, 383,112 road segments. Click commune: it becomes the source layer. In the Exploring area, tick Single selection, set the display expression to "nom_officiel" and type "Saint-Gaudens" in the list. The Tracking button of the Exploring column being on, the canvas recentres on the commune by itself.
  2. Tick every target. Filtering tab, Layers to filter row: right-click in the list, then Select All. The Intersect predicate is ticked, the Buffer row stays off.
  3. Filter. The log reports under a second for the twelve layers: on GeoPackage, FilterMate first queries the R-tree index of each table, then evaluates ST_Intersects on the candidates only.
  4. Configure the export. Exporting tab: the 12 layers, EPSG:2154, styles as QML, GPKG format, output folder and a ZIP archive.
  5. Export. Because the format is GeoPackage and several layers are exported, FilterMate shows a recap and offers to keep the group hierarchy. Confirm: the file is written in 4 seconds with an embedded QGIS project.

Under the hood

Provider filter generated for the buildings layer, as read in Layer Properties → Source:

ROWID IN (SELECT id FROM "rtree_batiment_geometrie"
          WHERE minx <= 517706.0 AND maxx >= 511938.3
            AND miny <= 6231762.5 AND maxy >= 6222765.8)
AND ST_Intersects("geometrie", ST_MakeValid(ST_GeomFromText('Polygon ((…))', 2154)))
Going further. If polygons straddling the boundary get in the way, replace Intersect with Are within: only objects entirely inside the commune remain. On very detailed polygons, the centroid box to the right of the Layers to filter row speeds up the cascade further.

2Which buildings are near the main roads?

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Muret.

What this filter measures. These are teaching distances. The importance attribute does not establish a noise classification; the result measures neither noise nor exposed population. For a noise study, use the areas and categories from the official classification.

The need

Nadia explores buildings near the main roads of Muret, crossed by the A64 motorway and several departmental roads. Study distances of 300 m and 250 m, chosen by road importance, demonstrate a variable buffer. The result counts buildings and public-access facilities, not residents.

The FilterMate translation

After scoping on Muret, the source layer becomes troncon_de_route with the custom selection "importance" IN ('1', '2'). The buffer is an expression evaluated for each segment: 300 m for importance 1, 250 m for importance 2. The Flat buffer type avoids round ends at segment tips. Targets: batiment and erp.

Source
troncon_de_route
Selection
"importance" IN ('1', '2')
Targets
batiment, erp
Relation
Intersect, buffer by expression, Flat
Result
2,753 buildings, 78 public buildings
  1. Scope Muret. Same gesture as in story 1: commune as source, Muret in single selection, every layer as target, Intersect, Filter. Muret has 19,458 buildings, 354 public buildings and 5,512 road segments.
  2. Pick the main roads. Click troncon_de_route in the Layers panel: it becomes the source. Tick Custom selection and type "importance" IN ('1', '2'). 102 segments are kept out of the commune's 5,512.
  3. Buffer by expression. Targets batiment and erp, Intersect predicate. On the Buffer value row, open the data-defined property menu, choose Edit…, then enter the following in the expression editor: 
    CASE WHEN "importance" = '1' THEN 300
     WHEN "importance" = '2' THEN 250
     ELSE 100 END
    Confirm the editor. On the next row, open the Round / Flat / Square list and choose Flat.
  4. Filter.

Result

2,753 buildings and 78 public buildings lie in the buffer zones of the 102 segments of importance 1 and 2 of Muret, of which 2,196 buildings and 75 public buildings in the commune itself: the buffer reaches the neighbours, because a new spatial filter replaces the scope (see "One rule to know").

Going further. The same expression can read a study distance stored in a verified attribute. A purely geometric alternative is "largeur_de_chaussee" * 3. Segments with an empty width need a coalesce("largeur_de_chaussee", 3).

3Buildings near watercourses

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Grenade.

Proximity, not hazard. A 100 m or 300 m buffer represents neither flood extent nor flood frequency. To study flooding, use a documented hazard layer as the filter source.

The need

Julien explores the surroundings of watercourses in Grenade, at the confluence of the Garonne and the Save. He compares buildings within 300 m and 100 m of the water surfaces, then restores the first result with Undo.

The FilterMate translation

Source: surface_hydrographique, scoped on Grenade, with a multiple selection and Select All (77 surfaces). Targets: buildings, public buildings and roads, Intersect predicate, 300 m buffer. A second Filter at 100 m, then Undo in the action bar.

4Find crossings to inspect

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Toulouse Métropole.

Candidates to inspect. Cross finds geometric intersections in plan view. The results are not a complete bridge inventory: check structure types and vertical position. Identify flashes the source watercourses here, before roads are filtered.

The need

Karim manages the engineering structures of Toulouse Métropole: 37 communes, close to 99,000 road segments. He wants the inventory of the places where roads cross a permanent watercourse, segments and structures, to plan inspections.

The FilterMate translation

Scoping is done this time on the epci layer. Then the source becomes troncon_hydrographique with the custom selection "persistance" = 'Permanent', the targets troncon_de_route and construction_lineaire, and the Cross predicate, without buffer: a road running along the river is not kept, a road crossing it is.

Source
troncon_hydrographique
Selection
"persistance" = 'Permanent'
Targets
roads, linear structures
Relation
Cross, no buffer
Result
747 road segments, 364 structures
  1. Scope the EPCI. epci layer as source, "Toulouse Métropole" in single selection on "nom_officiel", every other layer as target, Intersect. The filter returns 98,687 road segments, 1,844 watercourse segments, 1,181 linear constructions and 70 touched communes.
  2. Keep only permanent watercourses. Source troncon_hydrographique, Custom selection "persistance" = 'Permanent': 691 segments.
  3. Check the selection on the map. Click Identify in the exploration toolbar: the 691 watercourse segments kept by the custom selection flash red. This visual check confirms the filter source before applying it to the roads.
  4. Cross. Targets troncon_de_route and construction_lineaire, Cross predicate alone, Buffer row off. Filter.

Result

747 road segments and 364 linear constructions (mostly bridges) cross one of the 691 permanent watercourse segments of the metropolis. The same computation done outside FilterMate with GEOS gives the same numbers.

Why not Intersect? With Intersect and a buffer of a few metres, every road running along a bank would be kept as well. Cross only keeps what goes across. The Touch predicate would only keep segments ending exactly on the bank.

5Clearing brush around homes

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Bagnères-de-Luchon.

The need

Élodie, at the town hall of Bagnères-de-Luchon, maps vegetation near homes using a study distance of 50 m. She distinguishes woods and forests from hedges to prepare a field review. This filter does not determine owners’ brush-clearing obligations.

The FilterMate translation

Source batiment with the custom selection "usage_1" = 'Résidentiel', targets zone_de_vegetation and haie, Intersect, 50 m buffer. Then a second, attribute filter on the vegetation layer turned source, combined with AND with the previous spatial filter.

Source
batiment
Selection
"usage_1" = 'Résidentiel'
Targets
zone_de_vegetation, haie
Relation
Intersect, 50 m buffer, then AND
Result
58 woods and forests, 48 hedges
  1. Scope Luchon. 3,699 buildings, 269 vegetation zones, 123 hedges.
  2. Keep only dwellings. Source batiment, Custom selection "usage_1" = 'Résidentiel': 1,619 buildings.
  3. 50 m. Targets zone_de_vegetation and haie, Intersect, Buffer 50. Filter: 119 vegetation zones and 48 hedges remain.
  4. Keep only woods and forests. Click zone_de_vegetation in the Layers panel: it becomes the source. Custom selection: 
    "nature" IN ('Bois', 'Forêt fermée de feuillus', 'Forêt fermée de conifères',
             'Forêt fermée mixte', 'Forêt ouverte')
    Enable the Combine operator row and leave AND on the left: the new filter narrows the spatial filter already applied to the source layer instead of replacing it.
  5. Filter.

Result

Out of the commune's 269 vegetation zones, 119 touch the 50 m band around a dwelling, among which 58 woods and forests; 48 hedges are in the same case.

Under the hood

The vegetation layer's filter after step 5 keeps the spatial clause and adds the attribute clause:

(ROWID IN (SELECT id FROM "rtree_zone_de_vegetation_geometrie" WHERE …)
 AND ST_Intersects("geometrie", ST_Buffer(ST_MakeValid(ST_GeomFromText('MultiPolygon (…)', 2154)), 50.0)))
AND ("nature" IN ('Bois', 'Forêt fermée de feuillus', 'Forêt fermée de conifères', 'Forêt fermée mixte', 'Forêt ouverte'))

6Hamlets far from paved roads

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Aspet.

Geometric distance. This result measures neither travel time nor access for emergency vehicles. Check accessibility using the road network, traffic restrictions and field observations.

The need

At the fire station of Aspet, in the Pyrenean foothills, the response plan is being prepared: which hamlets and activity zones lie more than 200 m from a paved road, as candidates for a field check?

The FilterMate translation

This is the Disjoint predicate with a 200 m buffer: keep what shares no point with the buffer zone. Since Disjoint keeps everything far away, including the rest of the department, the target layers are first scoped by an attribute filter on the INSEE code, applied with FilterMate's Processing algorithm. The second filter then combines with AND.

Source
troncon_de_route
Selection
paved roads
Targets
hamlets, activity zones
Relation
Disjoint, 200 m buffer, AND
Result
22 hamlets
  1. Scope Aspet spatially for roads, buildings and vegetation (story 1), then by attribute for the two target layers: Toolbox → FilterMate → Filter multiple layers, layers zone_d_habitation and zone_d_activite_ou_d_interet, expression "insee_commune" = '31020'. 87 hamlets and 31 activity zones.
  2. Keep only paved roads. Source troncon_de_route, Custom selection "nature" NOT IN ('Chemin', 'Sentier', 'Escalier', 'Piste cyclable', 'Route empierrée'): 494 segments out of 1,168.
  3. Disjoint, 200 m, AND. Targets zone_d_habitation and zone_d_activite_ou_d_interet, Disjoint predicate alone, Buffer 200, Combine operator row on with AND on the right (the target layers' operator).
  4. Filter.

Result

22 hamlets of Aspet are more than 200 m from a paved road; none of the 31 activity zones is. At 100 m they would be 32.

Disjoint without scope. Without the attribute filter of step 1, Disjoint would have returned the 31,599 hamlets of the department that do not touch the 200 m band around Aspet's roads. An exclusion predicate always requires bounding the targets first.

7The team's query library

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Grenade.

Favorite name in the recordings. “PPRI Garonne 300 m” is the historical recording label. Prefer “Garonne proximity — 300 m”: this favorite stores a buffer, not a flood hazard zone.

The need

Julien (story 3) will redo the 300 m band at every BD TOPO update and wants his colleagues to replay it without him. The filter must exist outside his head: named, described, reapplicable in one click, exportable.

The FilterMate translation

A favorite records the complete filter: source layer, selection, predicates, buffer, target layers. The menu opens from the ★ badge in the header; the manager lets you rename, describe, change the scope (this project or all projects) and export as JSON. With the sharing extension, a shared git repository becomes the team library.

  2. Unfilter. The three layers get the whole department back: 1,173,829 buildings.
  3. Replay. ★ badge then "PPRI Garonne 300 m": the filter reapplies, with the same 14,426 buildings, 178 public buildings and 6,310 segments.

8Objects modified since a date

BD TOPO® 3.5 © IGN, Haute-Garonne.

Result scope. The filter reads date_modification in the current edition. It does not compare two editions or detect objects deleted between them.

The need

Sophie administers the department's GIS and has just received the March 2026 edition of the BD TOPO. Before publishing it, she wants to isolate objects whose modification date is on or after 15 March 2025 on four layers, for a quality check, and be able to redo that check in a Processing model at every edition.

The FilterMate translation

No geometry here: a single attribute filter to apply to several layers. That is the job of the filtermate:batch_filter algorithm of the Processing toolbox, usable in the graphical modeler, in batch mode or from Python.

9Parcels on steep slopes

LiDAR HD and BD TOPO® © IGN, Etalab cadastre, Estavar (Pyrénées-Orientales).

The need

At the town hall of Estavar, in Cerdagne, the local plan is being revised: which parcels of the village lie mostly on slopes above 30 %, hard to build on and prone to gullying?

The FilterMate translation

With mnt as the current layer, EXPLORING shows its raster tools. Compute on switches to Slope (%): the slope is computed on the fly from the DTM, without an intermediate raster. The value range 30 % → maximum, read on the slope histogram, becomes the source geometry. FILTERING keeps the parcels covered at least 50 % by it.

Source
mnt DTM, computed slope (%)
Selection
Slope range ≥ 30 %
Targets
parcelle
Relation
Intersect, coverage ≥ 50 %
Result
58 parcels
  1. Compute the slope. Click mnt in the Layers panel, then Compute on → Slope (%). The histogram now shows the slope.
  2. Pick the range. Tick Value range and type 30 in Min. The share of kept pixels shows under the histogram: 12.9 %.
  3. Relate to the parcels. Target parcelle, Intersect predicate, cov. ≥ 50 % next to the predicates.
  4. Filter.

Result

58 parcels of the square kilometre have at least half of their area on slopes above 30 %. The same filter on a precomputed slope raster (gdaldem slope -p) gives the same count.

Slope is not hazard. A steep slope is not a hazard zone: cross-check with the risk prevention plans before any decision.

10Building heights, measured by LiDAR

LiDAR HD and BD TOPO® © IGN, Etalab cadastre, Estavar (Pyrénées-Orientales).

The need

The planning rules cap building heights. The permit office wants the buildings of the village that rise more than 9 m above natural ground, measured rather than declared.

The FilterMate translation

With the point cloud as the current layer, ATTRIBUTES gets a Classification = 6 (building) condition and a height above the DTM ≥ 9 m, computed by PDAL at the first filter. The footprint of the kept points is the source geometry; a 50 % coverage leaves out the terraced neighbours.

Source
LiDAR HD point cloud (COPC)
Selection
Classification = 6 and height ≥ 9 m
Targets
batiment
Relation
Intersect, coverage ≥ 50 %
Result
57 buildings
  1. The building points. Click lhd, tick ATTRIBUTES, + Condition, then Classification = 6. The eye button isolates them on the map.
  2. The height. Tick Height above DTM ≥, type 9 and pick the mnt DTM.
  3. Relate to the buildings. Target batiment, Intersect, cov. ≥ 50 %.
  4. Filter. The height takes 75 s on 20.4 million points, then the filter is instant.

Result

57 buildings out of 668 have at least half of their footprint under roof points more than 9 m high. BD TOPO lists 68 at 9 m or more: check the differences on site.

Natural ground. The DTM is interpolated from the ground points; on a slope the height measured downhill is larger.

13Vegetation on an RGB orthophoto

Raster → raster · schematic.

The need

The parks department is preparing a vegetation map. It has a colour infrared orthophoto to identify vegetation and wants to show those areas on the RGB orthophoto, which is easier for colleagues to read.

The FilterMate translation

The ortho_irc image is the source. In EXPLORING, the expression NDVI > 0.3 selects pixels. Their geometry clips the ortho_rvb target with Intersect: the target colours stay inside the mask and the other pixels become NoData.

Expected result
RGB orthophoto masked in a VRT
  1. Identify the bands. Choose ortho_irc as the current layer. In EXPLORING, open a colour-role menu and choose IRC if bands 1, 2 and 3 are near infrared, red and green respectively. Adjust the roles if the file uses a different order.
  2. Select vegetation. Check RASTER EXPRESSION, then choose Insert → Vegetation (NDVI > 0.3). For a colour infrared image with NIR in band 1 and red in band 2, the expression is: 
    ("ortho_irc@1" - "ortho_irc@2") / ("ortho_irc@1" + "ortho_irc@2") > 0.3
    Turn on the preview with the selection button and adjust the threshold by comparing the retained areas with the image.
  3. Link to the other image. In FILTERING, check ortho_rvb in Layers to filter, choose Intersect and turn off the buffer. For this first run, start with an unfiltered target and leave combination unchecked.
  4. Filter, then export. Hide the source to read the target alone. EXPORTING can write the result as GeoTIFF or COG; Undo restores the complete target orthophoto.

Expected result

An RGB layer clipped to the NDVI mask, with its style and identifier preserved. A threshold of 0.3 is a starting point to check against the season, shadows and image characteristics.

14LiDAR points beneath rooftops

Raster → point cloud · schematic.

The need

A consultancy is preparing a survey of several rooftops visible on an orthophoto. It wants to extract the LiDAR points beneath their footprints without tracing each boundary in a vector layer.

The FilterMate translation

The source is the ortho_rvb raster. In Clicked object mode, the pipette follows connected pixels whose colours resemble the clicked pixel. The retained objects become the source geometry; Intersect clips the lhd cloud to this footprint.

Source
ortho_rvb
Selection
Pipette, clicked object
Target
lhd
Relation
Intersect, no buffer
Expected result
Cloud clipped beneath the objects
  1. Find the rooftop. Choose ortho_rvb as the current layer and zoom to a roof whose colour stands out from its surroundings. Check the red, green and blue roles in EXPLORING.
  2. Detect the object. In the pipette, choose Clicked object, then click inside the roof. Adjust the tolerance while checking the outline on the map. Ctrl+click adds a sample for another part of the roof or another rooftop.
  3. Link to the cloud. In FILTERING, check lhd in Layers to filter. Choose Intersect, with no buffer. Start with an unfiltered target cloud to retain every return within the selected footprint.
  4. Filter and inspect. Hide the orthophoto to examine the points, then show it again to check their footprint. EXPORTING can deliver LAZ or COPC; Undo restores the complete cloud.

Expected result

A derived cloud limited to the footprint of the retained pixels. This is a spatial filter: every return within that footprint may remain, including ground or vegetation points. To keep only building-class points, then apply Classification = 6 to the resulting cloud.

Read the outline. A shadow or a change in material can split a rooftop into several objects. Check the selection on the image before clipping, especially when the orthophoto and LiDAR acquisition dates differ.

15The DTM beneath ground points

Point cloud → raster · schematic.

The need

A surveyor is preparing a terrain inspection. She has an interpolated DTM and the original classified cloud. She wants to restrict the raster to areas where ground points are present in the survey.

The FilterMate translation

The lhd cloud is the source. In EXPLORING, the Ground preset retains only class 2. The footprint of these points clips the mnt target raster with Intersect and no buffer.

Source
lhd
Selection
Classification = 2
Target
mnt
Relation
Intersect, no buffer
Expected result
DTM restricted to the ground footprint
  1. Read the classification. Choose lhd as the current layer. In EXPLORING, open CLASSES and check that class 2 – Ground contains points.
  2. Retain the ground. In the Selection menu, choose Ground. Check that only class 2 is selected, then use the selection button to preview the points on the map.
  3. Link to the DTM. Check mnt in Layers to filter, choose Intersect and turn off the buffer. Use an unfiltered target and leave combination unchecked.
  4. Filter, then read the raster. Hide the cloud to see the clipped DTM. EXPORTING writes the result as GeoTIFF or COG; Undo restores the complete DTM and the previous source state.

Expected result

A DTM VRT whose pixels outside the ground-point footprint are NoData. Existing elevations are preserved: this operation clips the DTM without interpolating it again.

Footprint resolution. The mask is built on a grid, at 2 m by default. It describes the presence of ground points at that resolution, without measuring the DTM’s vertical accuracy.

16Vegetation near power lines

Point cloud → point cloud · schematic.

The need

A network operator is preparing visits to wooded areas. Using a survey with classified wire points, she wants to extract high vegetation near the lines to plan inspections.

The FilterMate translation

Two point cloud layers have separate roles: lhd_reseau provides the wire points (classes 13 and 14); lhd_vegetation is first filtered to class 5. A 10 m buffer around the wire footprint then clips that target.

Source
lhd_reseau
Selection
Classes 13 and 14
Target
lhd_vegetation, class 5
Relation
Intersect, 10 m buffer
Expected result
Vegetation within the corridor
  1. Prepare two layers. Load the cloud twice in QGIS or duplicate its layer. Name them lhd_reseau and lhd_vegetation. Use a metric CRS for the 10 m buffer, such as Lambert-93 in mainland France. Check that classes 13 – Wire – guard or 14 – Wire – conductor, and class 5 – High vegetation, are present.
  2. Prepare the vegetation target. Leave “apply to other tiles in the dataset” disabled on both layers to keep their selections independent. Choose lhd_vegetation as the current layer. In CLASSES, check only class 5. Uncheck Layers to filter and click Filter to apply the selection to this layer alone.
  3. Select the wires. Switch to lhd_reseau. In CLASSES, check only the available classes 13 and 14. Use the selection button to inspect the points that will form the source footprint.
  4. Clip the corridor. In FILTERING, check lhd_vegetation, choose Intersect and set Buffer to 10 m. Click Filter: clipping preserves the class filter already applied to the target. Hide the source to examine the result, then export the target as LAZ or COPC.

Expected result

A high-vegetation layer restricted to a corridor around the wire-point footprint. The distance is measured in plan view, from a footprint on a grid, to identify areas to inspect. The actual three-dimensional clearance between branches and conductors requires further point cloud analysis.

Classification required. Classes 13 and 14 are not present in every survey. Choose a dataset with classified wire points to reproduce this recipe.

17Replaying these stories at home

Start with a small project.

The Toutens demo includes four layers, a QGIS project, a favorite and instructions in English and French. Its counts differ from the department-wide examples above.

Download the demo (ZIP, 203 KB)
  1. Download the BD TOPO of your department from the IGN Géoplateforme ("all themes", GeoPackage format, Lambert-93). The file ships with its spatial indexes: that is what makes the cascades fast.
  2. Load the layers named in each story. The styles embedded in the GeoPackage hide buildings beyond 1:25,000: disable scale-dependent visibility in the layer properties, or apply your own styles.
  3. Replace the commune names with yours. The fields used (nom_officiel, importance, persistance, usage_1, nature, insee_commune, date_modification) are those of BD TOPO 3.x, identical in every department.
  4. To reproduce the screenshots for cases 1 to 8, the repository script scripts/recipe_screenshots.py drives QGIS from end to end of each story, checks the counts and saves one image per step. Recordings for cases 9 to 12 are prepared by scripts/site_videos.py.
  5. For illustrated recipes 13 to 16, load the layers named in each recipe and follow the settings. The diagrams show expected results; areas and counts will depend on your data.
Verification. The counts of stories 1 to 8 were measured with FilterMate 5.0, whose engine keeps the source geometry exact. Those of stories 2, 3 and 4 were also recomputed independently with GEOS (PyQGIS, without FilterMate): the numbers are identical. Earlier versions of this page, measured with a source simplified by up to 100 m, differed (story 4: 1,112 road segments instead of 747).
